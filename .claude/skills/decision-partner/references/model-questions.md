# Mental models, as questions

Models are only useful as the question they provoke. Pick the two or three that bite
on this specific decision — never recite the list.

## Framing the problem

- **First principles** — What do you actually know here, versus what did you inherit as an assumption?
- **Root problem** — Is this the problem, or the symptom of one you're not naming?
- **Inversion** — What outcome are you trying to avoid? What guarantees it?
- **Map vs territory** — What's your model of this situation, and where is it a simplification?
- **The "surely" operator** — Which part of your case rests on "surely, obviously"? That's the weak joint.

## Weighing the odds

- **Base rates** — What happens to most people who do this? Why are you the exception?
- **Probabilistic thinking** — What odds would you actually put on it? Would you bet at those odds?
- **Black swan / tail risk** — What's the rare event that ruins this, and can you survive it?
- **Three sides of risk** — Odds of getting hit; average consequence; tail consequence. Which one are you under-weighting?
- **Margin of safety** — How much cushion, and does it survive double the worst case you imagined?
- **Randomness and luck** — If this works, how much of it would be luck? Would you know?

## Checking yourself

- **Confirmation bias** — What evidence have you gone looking for, and what have you avoided?
- **Circle of competence** — Is this inside the area where your judgment is proven?
- **Survivorship bias** — Who tried this and failed, and why don't you hear from them?
- **Intuition's range** — Is your gut trained on this domain, or an adjacent one?
- **Avoidable stupidity** — Stressed, distracted, hurried, in a group? Any of those and the odds of a dumb call spike.

## Second-order effects

- **Compound effect** — What does this look like repeated 50 times?
- **Lead domino** — What decisions does this force later that you haven't looked at?
- **Second-order thinking** — And then what? Ask it three times.
- **Chaos dynamics** — Is this a system where small differences blow up, making confident prediction a mistake?
- **Red Queen** — Does standing still here mean falling behind?

## Acting

- **Optionality** — What preserves the most future choices at the lowest cost?
- **Shoot bullets before cannonballs** — What's the small cheap test before the committing version?
- **Comparative advantage** — Is this the thing only you can do, or the thing you're merely able to do?
- **Parkinson's Law of Triviality** — Is the time spent here proportional to what's at stake, or is this a bikeshed?
- **Occam's / Hanlon's razor** — Is there a simpler explanation? Does this require anyone to be malicious?
