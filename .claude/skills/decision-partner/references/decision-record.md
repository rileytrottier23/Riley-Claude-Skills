# Decision record

Keep it short. The point is that a future version of Riley can tell what he actually
believed at the time, not reconstruct it after the fact.

```markdown
## <decision>            <date>

**Quadrant:** consequential / inconsequential × reversible / irreversible

**The real problem:** one sentence.

**Options considered:** including the ones rejected early, and why.

**Decision:** what he chose.

**Reasoning:** the two or three things that actually drove it — not the full case,
the load-bearing parts.

**What I expect to happen:** the prediction. Concrete enough to be wrong.
Include rough confidence ("70% this clears by March").

**What would tell me I was wrong:** a number, a date, or an event. Not a feeling.

**Tripwire:** the pre-committed action if that happens.

**Review on:** a date.
```

## Why the prediction matters most

Everything else is recoverable from memory. The prediction is not — hindsight rewrites
it silently, and a review without it grades the outcome instead of the decision.
